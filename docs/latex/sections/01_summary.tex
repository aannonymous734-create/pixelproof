\section{Executive summary}\label{sec:summary}
\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The problem.} An analyst receives a satellite image patch and must know two different things: was it really produced by the stated source and left untouched since (\emph{integrity}), and does its content show signs of manipulation (\emph{semantic truth}). These are separate questions, because a perfectly signed image can still have been faked before signing. PixelProof answers them on \textbf{two independent axes} and keeps both answers.
\end{keybox}

\textbf{Our answer, in three points.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \hl{Integrity is proven, not guessed.} Each patch is bound at the source to a signed claim (an Ed25519 signature over a SHA-256 digest of the pixels, plus product id, source, sensor and capture date) and to a signed, linked record of every processing step. The ground station trusts only registered public keys and pinned algorithms, remembers what it received (replay protection) and logs every decision in a hash chain.
  \item \hl{Content is assessed, honestly.} 48 explainable forensic features feed a gradient-boosted classifier, fused with a 74,116-parameter forensic CNN. The alarm threshold is calibrated on clean EuroSAT patches for a {{m:/target_fpr|pct0}} \targ{} false-alarm budget. The output is ``manipulated (type)'' or ``no traces found'', never ``authentic''.
  \item \hl{The verdict keeps both answers} (\cd{VERIFIED}, \cd{SIGNED_BUT_SUSPICIOUS}, \cd{ALTERED_AFTER_CAPTURE}, \cd{SUSPICIOUS}, \cd{UNVERIFIED_NO_TRACES}), quarantines anything doubtful, and writes every decision to a hash-chained audit log.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warnbox}
\textbf{Phase 2 work already measured (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}).} A retrained candidate detector, \emph{not shipped}, raises held-out v1 detection to \ext{75.2\%} and frozen held-out v2 to \ext{84.6\%} at similar false-alarm rates, at the cost of more false alarms on re-encoded images. It is reported separately; every headline number above stays the shipped model's.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Main limitation.} Smoothing of authentic patches (Gaussian blur, JPEG 2000) still raises false alarms: we do not yet tell benign smoothing from a resampling attack, so such a patch is quarantined for an analyst's review rather than released (Sections~\ref{sec:robust} and~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{warnbox}
