\section{Business model and potential partners}\label{sec:biz}
\begin{keybox}
\textbf{In one sentence.} PixelProof is an open, laptop-sized verification layer that sits between a source of Earth-observation imagery and the people who act on it; the people who need it first are the operators who deliver imagery and the analysts who cannot afford to act on a fake. Everything in this section is a \textbf{hypothesis to validate with partners}: no agreement, pilot or customer exists yet, and no price or market size is claimed.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Why the need is real in Tunisia}
Tunisia is building up its own Earth-observation capacity: its first governmental satellite, described by the national mapping and remote-sensing centre (CNCT) as a 6U CubeSat designed for Earth observation, is reported to be in development \cite{tnsat}, and a space association and regional remote-sensing bodies are already active around it \cite{tunsa}. Every new source of imagery is also a new place where provenance can be lost. Adding signed origin to a new ground segment from day one costs far less than retrofitting it after products are already in circulation, and the downstream decisions named by the challenge (agriculture and insurance, water management, disaster response) are exactly the ones where a silently altered patch does harm.

\textbf{Why this model fits the technology.} The cost structure is small because the system is CPU-only and open (Section~\ref{sec:feas}); the value proposition is the one thing a signature alone cannot give (Section~\ref{sec:trust}), a verdict that keeps proof and content assessment separate; and the revenue hypotheses rest on services around an open core, which matches how a verification layer is adopted: it has to be inspectable before anyone trusts it.

\subsection{Partnership roadmap}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pilot on a small, friendly source.} Sign the products of one educational or student mission and run the ground-side verifier and dashboard on them (the labs of Section~\ref{sec:labs} are the rehearsal).
  \item \textbf{Validate on real imagery.} With a remote-sensing body, score the detector on real Sentinel-2 or CubeSat products and re-measure the false-alarm budget, because EuroSAT is single-JPEG and 8-bit (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
  \item \textbf{Move toward users.} Take the quarantine and audit workflow to an insurer, a water authority or a disaster-response desk, and let their tolerance for false alarms set the threshold.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{What we would need from a partner:} a handful of real signed or signable products, a place to host a registry of public keys, and honest feedback on how many false alarms a human reviewer will accept.
